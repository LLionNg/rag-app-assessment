NORTHWIND FINANCIAL GROUP - EMPLOYEE POLICY HANDBOOK (EXTRACT)
This is a fictional handbook written as sample data for a retrieval exercise. No part of it describes a real company, and none of it should be relied on as advice. Version 4.2, issued by People Operations and reviewed annually; the Group Policy Committee owns the text and approves every change. The extract below covers the policies employees ask about most often: travel, expenses, working arrangements, information security, leave, conduct, and the controls specific to a regulated financial institution. Where a policy refers to a portal, a register, or a named team, those systems are equally fictional. Sections are self-contained by design so that each one can be retrieved and cited on its own.

INTERNATIONAL TRAVEL - APPROVAL AND PLANNING
All international business travel must be approved in advance by the employee's line manager and by the Regional Travel Desk. Requests are submitted through the Travel Portal at least fourteen calendar days before departure; requests filed later than seven days before departure require a written exception from a Managing Director. The request records the business purpose, the full itinerary, the expected total cost, and the name of the budget holder who will carry the charge, and it cannot be approved without all four. Employees travelling to a country rated "elevated risk" or above by the Group Security team must additionally complete the pre-departure security briefing and register their itinerary with Global Security before boarding. It is the traveller's own responsibility to confirm that their passport has at least six months of validity remaining and that any visa or entry permit is in hand before booking; the Travel Desk will advise on requirements but does not apply on the employee's behalf. Trips cancelled for business reasons are charged to the sponsoring department, and any refundable portion must be recovered through the Travel Portal rather than claimed as an expense.

INTERNATIONAL TRAVEL - CLASS OF SERVICE AND ACCOMMODATION
Economy class is the standard for all flights under seven hours of scheduled flying time. Premium economy is permitted for flights between seven and eleven hours, and business class is permitted only for flights exceeding eleven hours or where the employee is scheduled to attend a client meeting within twelve hours of landing. Connecting sectors are measured as a single journey where the layover is under four hours, so a trip is not split into shorter legs to reach a higher class. Hotel bookings must be made through the Travel Portal so that negotiated corporate rates apply; the nightly cap is USD 220 for Tier 1 cities and USD 150 elsewhere. The cap covers the room and local taxes but not meals, which fall under the per diem. Upgrades paid for with personal funds or personal loyalty points are permitted and are never reimbursed, and loyalty points earned on company travel remain the employee's own. Rail is the expected mode for city pairs under four hours of scheduled journey time, in standard class or its equivalent. Car hire requires prior approval from the Travel Desk and is limited to compact categories; ride-hailing and licensed taxis are reimbursable, while employees are asked not to drive personal vehicles on company business abroad.

INTERNATIONAL TRAVEL - PER DIEM AND INSURANCE
Employees on international assignment receive a per diem of USD 75 per full day and USD 40 per partial day, which covers meals and incidental expenses. The per diem is not a reimbursement and needs no receipts, but it is claimed in the Finance Portal alongside the trip so that the total cost of travel is visible in one place. The per diem is reduced by 40 percent for any day on which meals are provided by a client or conference host. Group travel insurance covers all employees automatically for trips of up to ninety consecutive days; longer assignments require an extension arranged by People Operations at least one month in advance. Cover includes medical treatment, emergency evacuation, and repatriation, and the twenty-four hour assistance number is printed on the digital travel card issued with every approved itinerary. Employees must not travel on a personal passport for company business without written approval from Group Compliance. Where a personal holiday is attached to a business trip, the insurance cover ends when the business portion ends, and the employee is expected to arrange their own cover for the remainder.

EXPENSE REIMBURSEMENT
Expense claims are submitted in the Finance Portal within thirty calendar days of the expense being incurred. Claims older than sixty days are rejected unless a Finance Director grants an exception. Every claim line above USD 25 requires a legible receipt or a scanned invoice; card statements alone are not accepted as evidence, because they show the amount but not what was bought. Foreign currency lines are converted at the rate applied by the card issuer on the transaction date, or at the published Group monthly rate where the employee paid in cash. Claims are approved by the employee's line manager, and a manager may not approve their own expenses; those route to the next level up. Reimbursement is paid with the next payroll run after approval, which is typically fifteen to twenty business days from submission. Alcohol, personal entertainment, traffic fines, and lost baggage that is already covered by travel insurance are not reimbursable. Finance samples approved claims each quarter for review, and a claim found to be deliberately inflated is treated as a conduct matter rather than an administrative error.

CORPORATE CARD USE
Corporate cards are issued to employees at grade 5 and above, and to any employee who is expected to travel internationally more than twice per year. The card is for business expenditure only and remains the property of the company. Personal spending on a corporate card must be reported to Finance within five business days and repaid immediately; repeated personal use is grounds for withdrawal of the card. Cash advances are blocked by default and can be enabled for a single trip on request from a line manager. Cardholders reconcile their statement in the Finance Portal each month, matching every transaction to a receipt and a business purpose, and a statement left unreconciled for two consecutive months triggers an automatic suspension of the card. A lost or compromised card must be reported to the card issuer and to Finance Operations within twenty-four hours, and the employee should not wait until the next business day to do so. Cards are cancelled automatically on the employee's last working day, so any outstanding claims should be submitted before then.

REMOTE AND HYBRID WORK
Most roles operate on a hybrid schedule of three days in the office and two days remote, with the in-office days agreed at team level so that each team overlaps for at least two common days. Fully remote arrangements are approved case by case by a department head and are reviewed every six months. Employees working remotely must be reachable during their team's core hours of 09:30 to 16:30 local time, and calendars should reflect where the employee is working on any given day. The company provides a laptop, a headset, and a display on request; employees are responsible for a workspace that is private enough for confidential calls and for a connection that supports video meetings. Roles that require supervised access to trading, payments, or customer records may be designated office-only by the department head, and that designation overrides the default hybrid pattern. Working from a country other than the employee's country of employment requires prior approval from People Operations and Group Tax, because it can create a tax presence for the company; such arrangements are capped at twenty working days per calendar year and are not a substitute for an international assignment.

INFORMATION SECURITY - DEVICES AND ACCESS
Company data is accessed only from company-managed devices with full-disk encryption and the current endpoint agent installed. Personal devices may be enrolled for email and calendar only, and enrolment allows the company to remove company data from the device remotely. Multi-factor authentication is mandatory on every system that supports it, and passwords are managed exclusively through the approved password manager rather than written down, reused, or shared with a colleague. Local administrator rights are granted only where a role genuinely requires them and are reviewed quarterly. Security patches are applied within the monthly maintenance window, and a device that has missed two consecutive windows loses access to company systems until it is brought up to date. Employees must never use public Wi-Fi without the corporate VPN, and screens must be locked whenever a device is left unattended, including at home. Access rights follow the joiners, movers, and leavers process: entitlements are granted on the principle of least privilege, are re-certified by the system owner every six months, and are removed on the employee's last working day.

INFORMATION SECURITY - HANDLING CUSTOMER DATA
Customer data is classified as Confidential or Restricted and may not be copied to personal storage, personal email, or unapproved cloud services or AI tools. Restricted data may only be processed inside approved systems within the customer's own jurisdiction, and moving it across a border requires a documented transfer assessment. Printing customer data is discouraged and, where unavoidable, requires secure print release and immediate collection; a clear desk at the end of each day applies in the office and at home. Screenshots of customer records must not be pasted into chat tools or attached to tickets. Sharing customer data with a third party requires an executed data protection agreement and the prior approval of the data owner. Any suspected data loss, phishing attempt, or unauthorised access is reported to the Security Operations Centre within one hour of discovery; employees are never penalised for reporting an incident in good faith, and late reporting causes far more harm than the original mistake. Retention periods are set by Group Records Management, and data is deleted through the approved disposal workflow rather than by the employee.

APPROVED USE OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE TOOLS
Employees may use generative AI tools only where the tool appears on the approved list maintained by Group Technology, and only through the enterprise accounts the company provides. Consumer accounts must not be used for any work purpose, because prompts submitted to them may be retained or used for model training outside the company's control. Customer data classified as Confidential or Restricted, unpublished financial results, and personal data about colleagues must never be entered into any AI tool, approved or otherwise. Output is treated as a draft: the employee who uses it remains accountable for accuracy, for any advice given to a customer, and for any code committed to a company repository. Where AI-assisted output materially shapes a customer communication, a credit decision, or a regulatory submission, its use is recorded so that the decision can be explained later. Group Technology reviews the approved list quarterly, and requests to add a tool are made through the Technology Service Portal with a description of the data the tool would process.

ANNUAL LEAVE
Full-time employees accrue twenty-two days of annual leave per year, rising to twenty-five days after five years of continuous service. Part-time employees accrue the same entitlement on a pro-rata basis. Leave is requested in the People Portal and requires line manager approval; requests of five consecutive days or more should be submitted at least three weeks in advance, and managers are expected to respond within five business days so that employees can book with confidence. Public holidays in the employee's country of employment are additional to this entitlement. Up to five unused days may be carried into the following year and must be taken before 31 March, after which they lapse; carry-over beyond five days is granted only where business demands prevented the employee from taking leave. Employees in client-facing control functions are required to take at least ten consecutive calendar days away from all systems each year, which is a control against undetected activity rather than a wellbeing measure, and their access is monitored during that period. Unused accrued leave is paid out in the final salary when employment ends.

SICK LEAVE AND MEDICAL ABSENCE
Employees notify their line manager as early as possible on the first day of absence and record the absence in the People Portal on return. A medical certificate is required for any absence of three or more consecutive working days. Paid sick leave is capped at thirty working days per calendar year, after which the case is referred to People Operations for a long-term absence review that considers phased return, adjusted duties, and any statutory entitlement. Where an absence is expected to be lengthy, People Operations may arrange an occupational health assessment, and any recommendations are agreed with the employee before they are put in place. An employee who falls ill during booked annual leave may convert those days back to sick leave with a medical certificate. Medical information is held by People Operations and is not shared with the line manager beyond fitness-to-work status, so managers should not ask for a diagnosis and should route any clinical detail they receive back to People Operations.

PARENTAL LEAVE
The primary caregiver is entitled to ninety-eight days of fully paid parental leave, and the secondary caregiver to fifteen paid working days that may be taken in up to three separate blocks within the first six months. The same entitlements apply to adoption and to surrogacy arrangements, with the placement date treated as the birth date for the purposes of the policy. Parental leave is available from the first day of employment with no qualifying service period. Employees returning from parental leave may request a phased return of up to eight weeks at reduced hours on full pay for the first four weeks. Notice of intended leave dates should be given at least eight weeks before the expected start date, using the People Portal, and the dates may be moved once without penalty. Paid time off is available for antenatal or adoption appointments, and employees on leave may agree up to ten keeping-in-touch days with their manager without bringing the leave to an end. Annual leave continues to accrue throughout parental leave.

COMPENSATION AND PAYROLL
Salaries are paid monthly on the last business day, with payslips available in the People Portal three days beforehand. The annual compensation review runs alongside the January performance review and any increase takes effect from 1 April. Variable pay is discretionary and depends on Group performance, department performance, and the individual's rating; it is never guaranteed. Employees identified as Material Risk Takers under the Group's remuneration policy have a portion of any award deferred over three years and subject to malus and clawback, and their awards require Remuneration Committee approval. Payroll errors should be raised with People Operations within thirty days of the payslip; an underpayment is corrected in the next run or, where the amount is material, by an off-cycle payment. Overpayments are recovered by agreement over a reasonable period. Salary information is confidential to the employee, their manager, and People Operations.

TRAINING AND PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT
Each employee has an annual development budget of USD 1,500 that may be spent on courses, conferences, and professional memberships, and it does not carry over into the next year. Spending is approved by the line manager in advance and claimed like any other expense. Professional certifications required by a role, such as risk or compliance qualifications, are funded separately and in full and do not consume the personal budget. Where the company funds a qualification costing more than USD 5,000, the employee agrees to a twelve-month retention period after completion, and a proportion is repayable if the employee resigns within that period. Study leave of up to five days per year is available for examinations. Separately, every employee completes the mandatory training curriculum each year, covering information security, financial crime, conduct, and data protection; completion is tracked in the learning system, and an overdue module is escalated to the department head and may affect the year-end rating.

PROCUREMENT AND SUPPLIER ENGAGEMENT
No employee may commit the company to a supplier contract without a purchase order raised in the Procurement Portal, and verbal commitments to a supplier carry no authority. Purchases up to USD 10,000 require department head approval; purchases above that threshold require three competitive quotes and a Procurement Committee review. Splitting a larger requirement into smaller orders to stay under the threshold is a breach of this policy. Only holders of delegated signing authority may sign a contract, and the current list is maintained by Group Legal. Any supplier that will process customer data must pass a security and data protection assessment before contracts are signed, and the assessment is repeated every two years or whenever the service changes materially. Suppliers judged critical to a business service are additionally subject to exit planning and annual continuity testing. Gifts or hospitality from suppliers with a value above USD 100 must be declined or declared in the Gifts Register, and gifts of cash or cash equivalents are declined at any value.

CODE OF CONDUCT AND CONFLICTS OF INTEREST
Employees declare any outside business interest, second employment, or close personal relationship with a supplier, competitor, or customer in the Conflicts Register, and update the declaration within thirty days of any change. An outside directorship requires prior written approval, and approval may be withdrawn if the role later conflicts with the employee's duties. Personal trading in securities is subject to the Group Personal Account Dealing rules, which require pre-clearance and a minimum thirty-day holding period; employees on the restricted list may not deal in the affected instruments at all. The Group operates a zero-tolerance policy on bribery and facilitation payments, and no business objective justifies an exception. Political donations are never made in the company's name. Concerns may be raised anonymously through the Speak Up line, which is operated by an independent third party, and retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith is itself a disciplinary matter.

FINANCIAL CRIME, SANCTIONS AND CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE
Every customer relationship is subject to due diligence before onboarding and to periodic review thereafter, at a frequency set by the customer's risk rating. No account is opened and no transaction is processed where identification and verification remain incomplete. Employees who see activity inconsistent with what they know of a customer raise an internal suspicion report to the Money Laundering Reporting Officer immediately and do not investigate it themselves. Employees must never tell a customer, or anyone outside the reporting chain, that a report has been made, as tipping off is a criminal offence in most of the jurisdictions in which the Group operates. Sanctions screening runs at onboarding and on every payment, and an alert must be cleared by the Financial Crime team rather than by the business. Records supporting due diligence are retained for the period set by Group Records Management. Mandatory financial crime training is completed annually, and a failure to complete it is treated as a conduct matter.

PERFORMANCE AND PROMOTION
Performance is reviewed twice a year, with a mid-year check-in in July and a full review in January covering both objectives and behaviours. Objectives are agreed within thirty days of the start of the year, or of joining, and are visible to both the employee and the manager in the People Portal throughout. Ratings are calibrated at department level before they are shared with employees, so that a rating means the same thing across teams. Promotion nominations are made by the line manager during the January cycle and require evidence of sustained performance at the higher grade for at least two review periods. Employees on a formal performance improvement plan are not eligible for promotion until the plan has been closed successfully for one full review period. An employee who disagrees with a rating may ask for it to be reviewed by their manager's manager within fourteen days, and People Operations attends that discussion.

ONBOARDING AND PROBATION
New employees serve a probation period of six months, which may be extended once by up to three months where more time is needed to assess performance, with the reasons given in writing. Notice during probation is two weeks on either side. Access to systems is granted on the first day against the role profile, and nothing beyond that profile is granted without a documented request. The mandatory training curriculum covering information security, financial crime, conduct, and data protection is completed within the first thirty days, and access to customer systems is withdrawn if it is not. Managers hold a documented check-in at the end of each of the first three months, and confirmation of appointment is recorded in the People Portal before the probation period ends. An employee whose probation is not confirmed by the final date is treated as confirmed by default, so managers are expected to act well before the deadline.

GRIEVANCE, DISCIPLINARY AND SPEAK UP
Employees are encouraged to raise concerns informally with their line manager first, because most matters are resolved fastest that way. Where that is not appropriate or has not worked, a formal grievance is submitted in writing to People Operations, who acknowledge it within five business days and aim to conclude the process within thirty. Disciplinary matters follow the same principles: the employee is told the substance of the concern in writing before any meeting, is given a reasonable opportunity to respond, and may be accompanied by a colleague. Sanctions range from a written warning to dismissal, and the decision letter always sets out the right of appeal and the deadline for exercising it. Serious misconduct, including breaches of the financial crime, conduct, or information security policies, may result in summary dismissal. Concerns about wrongdoing that the employee does not wish to raise by name go to the independent Speak Up line, and every report is triaged by Group Compliance regardless of how it arrives.

HEALTH, SAFETY AND WELLBEING
The company provides a confidential Employee Assistance Programme, available at no cost to employees and their immediate household, offering counselling and practical advice twenty-four hours a day. Use of the programme is not visible to the employer. Employees complete a display screen equipment assessment on joining and whenever their regular workstation changes, including a move to home working, and equipment identified as necessary is provided. Accidents, near misses, and unsafe conditions are recorded in the Safety Register on the day they occur, however minor they appear. Fire evacuation drills are held twice a year at each office and participation is not optional. Managers are expected to monitor workload and working hours, and an employee who is consistently working beyond their contracted hours should raise it in their one-to-one; sustained excessive hours are treated as a resourcing problem for the manager to solve rather than a matter of individual resilience.

BUSINESS CONTINUITY AND INCIDENT RESPONSE
Every business service has a documented continuity plan naming the accountable owner, the maximum tolerable outage, and the alternative way of working to be used while normal service is unavailable. Plans are tested at least annually, and the results, including anything that failed, are reported to the Operational Resilience Committee. During an incident, the incident manager owns all communication; individual teams do not contact customers or regulators directly, and no employee comments publicly or on social media about an incident. Employees in roles identified as critical keep current contact details in the People Portal and may be asked to work from an alternative site or remotely at short notice. Technology incidents are raised through the Technology Service Portal, and anything with a suspected security dimension goes to the Security Operations Centre at the same time. After a significant incident, a blameless review is held within ten business days and its actions are tracked to closure.

LEAVING THE COMPANY
Notice periods are set out in the employment contract and are typically one month for grades below 5 and three months at grade 5 and above. Resignations are submitted in writing to the line manager, who informs People Operations the same day so that the offboarding checklist can begin. All company property, including laptops, phones, access cards, and any printed material, is returned on or before the last working day, and system access is revoked at the end of that day. Outstanding expense claims must be submitted before the final day, as the corporate card is cancelled at the same time. Final salary includes any unused accrued annual leave and is paid in the normal payroll run following the leaving date. Obligations of confidentiality, and any restrictions on soliciting customers or colleagues, continue after employment ends. People Operations offers every leaver an exit conversation, which is voluntary and reported only in aggregate.
